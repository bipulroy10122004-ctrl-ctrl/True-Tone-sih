2. Digital Signal Processing (DSP) for Beginners

Before understanding machine learning for audio, you must understand what sound actually is when stored on a computer.

2.1 Sound Waves & Sampling Rate

Sound is a physical pressure wave that travels through air. A microphone contains a diaphragm that vibrates when hit by sound waves, converting air pressure changes into continuous electrical voltage. A computer cannot store infinite continuous voltage, so it must perform Analog-to-Digital Conversion (ADC) by measuring (sampling) the voltage at regular time intervals.

The Nyquist-Shannon Sampling Theorem:
To accurately capture a sound wave of frequency $f_{\text{max}}$, you must sample the audio at a rate of at least $2 \times f_{\text{max}}$.
f_s \ge 2 \cdot f_{\text{max}}
Human speech spans frequencies up to approximately 8,000 Hz (8 kHz). Therefore, True Tone standardizes all incoming audio to 16,000 Hz (16 kHz). Sampling at 16,000 samples per second preserves all speech frequencies up to 8,000 Hz while eliminating unnecessary high-frequency file size bloat.

2.2 Time Domain vs. Frequency Domain

When you record 4 seconds of audio at 16,000 Hz, your computer stores a simple 1D array of 64,000 floating-point numbers ($4 \times 16,000 = 64,000$). This is the time domain: it tells you how loud the signal was at each fraction of a millisecond, but it cannot tell you which musical notes or pitch frequencies were present!

The Smoothie Analogy (Fourier Transform)

A time-domain audio wave is like a delicious fruit smoothie: all ingredients (strawberries, bananas, milk) are mixed into one single liquid. The Fourier Transform is a mathematical machine that takes the smoothie and separates it back into individual bowls of strawberries, bananas, and milk. It breaks a complex audio wave into pure sine waves of distinct frequencies!

2.3 Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) & Spectrograms

Human speech changes rapidly over time. A person says "Hello", which contains different phonemes ('H', 'eh', 'l', 'o') every few milliseconds. To analyze frequencies over time, we use the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT):

  • Framing: Chop the 64,000-sample audio array into small, overlapping chunks called frames (typically 25 milliseconds long = 400 samples at 16kHz).
  • Hop Step: Slide the window forward by 10 milliseconds (160 samples), creating smooth continuity between frames.
  • Windowing: Multiply each frame by a Hamming window so the edges taper smoothly to zero, preventing false frequency artifacts (spectral leakage).
  • FFT: Compute the Fast Fourier Transform on each 25ms frame.

The result is a 2D image called a Spectrogram, where the X-axis is Time, the Y-axis is Frequency, and the brightness/color represents Energy (loudness).

3. Machine Learning Theory for Audio Classification

If you are new to Machine Learning, this section explains the exact mental models required to understand how computers learn to distinguish human voices from AI fakes.

3.1 What is Machine Learning?

In traditional programming, a human engineer writes explicit rules: if amplitude > 0.8: return "SPOOF". In Machine Learning, we don't write rules. Instead:

  1. We collect thousands of examples: 25,000 genuine human audio recordings and 25,000 synthetic AI-generated audio clips.
  2. We define a mathematical model with millions of adjustable parameters (called weights, $W$, and biases, $b$).
  3. We show the model an audio clip. The model makes a guess (e.g. 85% spoof probability).
  4. We calculate the difference between the model's guess and the true label using a Loss Function.
  5. Using calculus (Backpropagation and Gradient Descent), we nudge each weight slightly to make the model more accurate on the next attempt.

3.2 Why Not Use MFCC? (The Critical Audio Spoofing Insight)

In standard speech recognition (Siri, Alexa), engineers use MFCC (Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients). The Mel scale mimics human ears by clustering lots of filters in low frequencies (0 – 1,000 Hz) where human speech vowels live, and compressing high frequencies (4,000 – 8,000 Hz).

Why MFCC Fails for Deepfake Detection:
AI voice synthesizers and neural vocoders (like HiFi-GAN and MelGAN) sound very natural to human ears in the low frequencies. However, their mathematical generation algorithms introduce microscopic phase discontinuities and robotic spectral artifacts in the high linear frequency bands (4 kHz – 8 kHz).
MFCC discards these high-frequency details! If you use MFCC for spoof detection, the model is practically blind to vocoder artifacts.

4. Linear Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (LFCC)

True Tone utilizes Linear Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (LFCC). Unlike MFCC, LFCC spaces its triangular filters linearly across the entire frequency range from 0 Hz to 8,000 Hz, preserving the critical high-frequency vocoder signatures.

1
Pre-Emphasis Filter

Human speech has a natural spectral tilt: low frequencies carry high acoustic energy, while high frequencies carry very low energy. We apply a finite impulse response (FIR) filter:

y[t] = x[t] - \alpha \cdot x[t-1] \quad (\alpha = 0.97)

This amplifies high-frequency energy by up to 20 dB, making subtle synthetic artifacts easily visible to neural network layers.

2
Linear Triangular Filterbank & Log Energy

We place 40 equally spaced triangular bandpass filters from 0 Hz to 8,000 Hz. For each 25ms frame, we multiply the FFT power spectrum by these 40 filters and compute the natural logarithm of the energy:

E_m = \ln \left( \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} |X[k]|^2 \cdot H_m[k] \right)

The logarithm mimics human hearing (which perceives loudness logarithmically) and compresses dynamic range.

3
Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT-II)

Adjacent filterbank energies are strongly correlated with each other. We apply DCT-II to decorrelate them and extract the first 20 static cepstral coefficients ($c_0$ through $c_{19}$):

c_n = \sum_{m=0}^{M-1} E_m \cdot \cos \left( \frac{\pi n (m + 0.5)}{M} \right)
4
Delta (Δ) & Delta-Delta (ΔΔ) Temporal Derivatives

Static coefficients tell us the vocal tract shape at an instant. But human speech is dynamic! Real human vocal cords accelerate and change pitch smoothly according to human muscle biomechanics, while AI synthesizers often have unnatural instantaneous jumps. We calculate:

  • Velocity (Δ): First-order derivative over time ($d_t$).
  • Acceleration (ΔΔ): Second-order derivative over time ($d'_t$).

Total Feature Dimensions: 20 Static + 20 Delta + 20 Delta-Delta = 60 Features per time frame!

src/audio/features.py (Core LFCC Extraction) 
def extract_lfcc(waveform, sample_rate=16000, num_cepstral=20, num_filters=40, target_frames=400):
    # 1. Pre-emphasis filtering (boost high frequencies where vocoder anomalies live)
    emphasized = waveform[:, 1:] - 0.97 * waveform[:, :-1]
    
    # 2. Short-Time Fourier Transform (25ms window, 10ms hop step)
    stft = torch.stft(emphasized, n_fft=512, hop_length=160, win_length=400, window=torch.hamming_window(400))
    power_spec = stft.pow(2).sum(-1)
    
    # 3. Apply 40 linear triangular filterbanks
    filterbank = get_linear_filterbank(num_filters, n_fft=512, sr=sample_rate)
    linear_energies = torch.matmul(filterbank, power_spec)
    log_energies = torch.log(torch.clamp(linear_energies, min=1e-10))
    
    # 4. Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) -> 20 static coefficients
    static_lfcc = apply_dct(log_energies)[:num_cepstral]
    
    # 5. Compute Velocity (Delta) and Acceleration (Delta-Delta)
    delta = compute_delta(static_lfcc)
    delta_delta = compute_delta(delta)
    
    # Final stacked 60-dimensional matrix: (Batch, 400 frames, 60 features)
    features = torch.cat([static_lfcc, delta, delta_delta], dim=1)
    return pad_or_truncate(features, target_frames)

5. Deep Learning Model Architecture

Layer 2: Temporal Sequence Memory

Processes speech over time in two directions (forward and backward). Understands how phonemes flow naturally from past into future.

Scans all 100 compressed time frames and calculates a dynamic attention weight. Focuses on the exact milliseconds where acoustic glitches occur.

Deep Dive for Beginners: How does LSTM Memory work?

Standard neural networks have no memory: each input frame is processed in total isolation. But speech is inherently sequential: whether a sound is natural depends heavily on what was said 100 milliseconds earlier!

An LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) network solves this using a specialized memory cell guarded by three mathematical gates:

  • Forget Gate ($f_t$): Decides what information from previous words to throw away.
  • Input Gate ($i_t$): Decides what new acoustic details to store into memory.
  • Output Gate ($o_t$): Decides what information to output to the next layer.

Because True Tone uses a Bidirectional LSTM, the network reads the speech sentence from left-to-right AND right-to-left simultaneously, catching inconsistencies in phonetic transitions regardless of sentence position.

6. Loss Functions & Evaluation Metrics

How do we measure if an audio anti-spoofing model is actually good? Accuracy alone is misleading because security datasets are often imbalanced. We use international biometric standards.

6.1 Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) Loss

During training, the model outputs a probability $\hat{y} \in [0, 1]$ where $y = 0$ is authentic human voice and $y = 1$ is an AI spoof. The loss function penalizes confident wrong guesses exponentially:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}} = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \left[ y_i \ln(\hat{y}_i) + (1 - y_i) \ln(1 - \hat{y}_i) \right]

6.2 The Biometric Tradeoff: FAR vs. FRR

Every biometric classification decision uses a decision threshold $\theta \in [0, 1]$:

  • If probability $\ge \theta$: Classify as AI SPOOF.
  • If probability $< \theta$: Classify as GENUINE HUMAN.
False Alarm Rate (FAR / Miss Rate)

An AI Deepfake is Missed

A synthetic AI voice is falsely accepted as a genuine human. In banking, this allows a fraudster to steal money.

False Rejection Rate (FRR / False Alarm)

A Real Human is Blocked

A legitimate customer is falsely accused of being an AI deepfake. Their call is dropped, creating customer frustration.

6.3 Equal Error Rate (EER)

If you make the threshold very high ($\theta = 0.95$), you almost never block real humans, but fraudsters slip through easily. If you make it very low ($\theta = 0.10$), you catch all fraudsters, but you block innocent customers.

Definition of Equal Error Rate (EER):
The Equal Error Rate (EER) is the exact operational threshold where the False Acceptance Rate equals the False Rejection Rate ($\text{FAR} = \text{FRR}$).
Lower is better. True Tone achieved an EER of 0.0022% (0.000022) on the ASVspoof 2019 benchmark dataset.

Most academic AI deepfake projects stop at classification: they output a number on screen and do nothing else. In a real cyberattack, an automated botnet can make 10,000 VoIP calls per hour. True Tone SIH integrates an Active Network Firewall that closes the loop between machine learning and network perimeter security.

Tier 1: High Velocity In-Memory Cache

Sub-Microsecond Lookups

A thread-safe Python dictionary in RAM mapping ip -> expiration_datetime. Checks incoming connections in less than 0.05 milliseconds without hitting disk storage during high-volume call floods.

Tier 2: Persistent ACID SQLite Storage

Durable Audit Trail

Stores quarantined IPs, strike counts, threat scores, and forensic event details in models/security_firewall.db. Retains state across server reboots.

9.1 Multi-Frame Strike Hysteresis

In real-world telephony, a microphone can click, or a person might cough. If a security firewall banned a caller after a single anomalous audio frame, legitimate customers would frequently get disconnected. True Tone enforces a strict 2-Strike State Machine:

1
Strike 1: Elevated Threat Detected

Status transitions to THREAT_SUSPECTED (ANALYZING_PHONEMES). The system logs an internal security alert and increases acoustic monitoring scrutiny, but allows the call to continue.

2
Strike 2: Sustained Synthetic Speech Confirmed

Status transitions to THREAT_NEUTRALIZED. The WebSocket session is terminated (code 4403), the caller IP is instantly quarantined in the firewall with a 3600-second TTL, and a broadcast alert appears on the Command Center dashboard.
